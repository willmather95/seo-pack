# Local SEO

One job: win the map pack for the services that make money, inside the radius customers will travel.

**Does it apply?** Only if the business has a real address or service area and a Business Profile. Online-only sites and newsletters skip the profile and reviews parts. They can still use the NAP and local links sections.

## 1. Map pack first
The map often moves in days; organic rankings take weeks. Work the profile before the pages. Everything listed here is a draft or a checklist. Editing the live profile or listings needs the owner's yes.

Profile checklist:
- **Primary category:** the one that matches the service you most want booked. It carries the most weight.
- **Secondary categories:** only for services that bring real revenue.
- **Services:** every service as its own item, 1-2 sentence description, starting price if the owner is fine sharing it.
- **Photos:** real rooms, front desk, outside of the building, team. Add new ones every few weeks.
- **Booking link:** straight to online booking, set to the right service where possible.
- **Website link:** to the matching service page, not the homepage, when the profile is for one main service.
- **Hours:** accurate, holidays included. Show evening and weekend hours if offered.
- **Posts and Q&A:** a short monthly batch drafted from real offers and real questions.

## 2. Citations and NAP
- The business name, address and phone must match everywhere, to the character ("Suite" vs "Ste").
- Order: Google, Apple Maps, Bing, Yelp, Facebook, then industry directories, then brand "find a provider" pages for products the business carries. Then supplier directories and government business or supplier profiles the business is really eligible for.
- Audit prompt (paste a real sheet):
```
Here is every place [business] is listed [URL, name shown, address shown, phone shown, link yes/no].
Flag every mismatch against the canonical record: [name, address, phone, URL]. Flag mentions with no link.
Sort by prominence using only the traffic or DR column I supplied.
```
- Fixing listings is manual and needs the owner's yes.

## 3. Reviews
- A steady flow beats a big old count. Recent reviews count for more.
- Ask in person at the end of the visit or when the job closes, then send the direct review link the same day, with 2-3 short reminders a few days apart. People who say yes forget.
- Track new reviews per month. A flat month is a defect to fix, not a stat to report.
- It helps if the customer names the service in their own words. Never write a review for them, never pay or gate reviews.
- Reply to every review within 1-2 days.
- Read the words, not just the stars. Jev sorts each review (ours and the 3 map-pack rivals): names service and place / names service / generic praise / complaint / other (jev-seo-campaign, local). Code finds services named in their reviews but never in ours. Then improve the review ask. Never put keywords in owner replies.

**Privacy-safe replies (health, dental, legal, finance):** never confirm the person is a customer or patient, never mention their treatment, visit or case. Keep it warm and general. For a negative review, keep it short and take it offline ("We'd like to talk this through. Please call our office."). Real case: in 2019 a Dallas dental practice paid HHS $10,000 after replying to Yelp reviews with patient details. Regulated firms (law, advisers) also follow their own bar or compliance rules. A person approves replies to negative reviews.

Reply prompt:
```
Write a warm reply under 50 words to this review: [review]. Do not confirm they are a customer or patient. Do not mention any service, visit, case or detail. If negative, invite them to call the office. No em dashes.
```

## 4. Local links
Earn them: local news and magazines, chambers and associations, sponsored community events, nearby partner businesses, suppliers. No bought links. Hand targets to seo-authority's links lane.

## 5. AI answers for local
Chatbots and AI Overviews answering "best [service] in [city]" pull from the profile, reviews, the website and big directories. The same work that wins the map tends to get the business named. Fill in the Yelp profile fully (categories, hours, photos, services) with the same NAP, since chatbots lean on it for local answers. Check who gets named for 3-5 money queries each month and log the cited sources.

## 6. Track what matters
- Calls, bookings and direction requests from the profile.
- **Map rank on a grid** of points around the location, not one check from the office. Being #1 at the front door and invisible two miles away is common.
- Rank for each service + city query.
- Booked consults or leads from the website.
- New reviews per month.
Bookings are the score. Rankings are how you get there.

## 7. Timeline to set expectations
- Month 1: profile rebuilt, citations cleaned, service pages going live. Calls and profile views start to rise.
- Months 2-3: map rank climbs. Service pages reach pages 1-2.
- Months 3-6: organic catches up. Competitive metros take the full 6 months or more.
These are expectations from practice, not promises. Never quote them to a client as a guarantee.
